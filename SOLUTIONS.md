## Act I — The Sluice Gate (3 wheels)

The slate on the wall beside the gate says plainly what it wants:

> I. the MONTH of the great tide
> II. the DEPTH of the sump, in fathoms, as the GAUGE reads it now
> III. the LAST figure of the minutes past the turn of high water

Three sources, three figures, and no single one of them gives you all three.

**Which tide?** The keeper's logbook, found on the desk in the east alcove under the
fallen beam, says on the 6th of March: *"It came higher this season than any day of it.
I barred the sluice at the turn of the tide and let it run."* So the "great tide" is
the highest water in the tide table on the west wall — and the greatest height in that
table is **7.6 fathoms on the 6th of March, at 06:42**.

**I — the month.** March is **3**.

**III — the last figure of the minutes.** 06:42 — take the final digit: **2**.

**II — the depth.** Not 7.6. The table's heights are *at the Bell*, and says so in its
own footnote: *"THEY ARE NOT THE SUMP. FOR THE SUMP, READ THE GAUGE."* The graduated
brass gauge on the north-east pier has its float resting at **VII** — **7**.

The logbook even warns you: *"The gauge reads true. Trust the brass and not my
arithmetic."*

### **3 · 7 · 2**

The gate rises and the sump drains over about eight seconds. Until it does, the trench
beyond is too deep to wade — the water is the lock.

## Act II — The Meridian Instrument (3 rings)

The plate bolted to the orrery gives the rule:

> THE MERIDIAN GATE ANSWERS TO THE THREE FIXED STARS THAT ROSE WITHIN THE MERIDIAN
> SPAN. SET THE RINGS IN THE ORDER THEY ROSE.

The star chart — a great painted board lit on the east wall — tabulates all ten figures
and their rising bearings, and marks the meridian span on its compass rose as
**090° ± 5**.

Three bearings fall inside 090° ± 5:

| | rising azimuth |
|---|---|
| The Glass | 087° |
| The Pendulum | 090° |
| The Compass | 094° |

"In the order they rose" means ascending bearing: **Glass, Pendulum, Compass** — rings
I, II, III.

Note that the logbook in Act I is what tells you what the span *is*: *"The meridian span
is five degrees either side of due east, and nothing outside it will pass."* The chart
happens to mark the same arc, so it can be solved without it, but you have to be told
the phrase exists somewhere before "the meridian span" means anything.

### **Glass · Pendulum · Compass**

The instrument takes up, the whole tower begins to turn, and the meridian gate opens.

## Act III — The Vault (4 wheels)

The lens plate beside the wheels:

> SET THE VAULT TO THE YEAR OF COMPLETION, IN PLAIN FIGURES.
> MDCCC = 1800 · L = 50 · XXX = 30 · IX = 9
> RECORD THE YEAR OF THE INSTRUMENT. NOT THE YEAR OF THE REIGN.

The foundation stone in the dark north-west corner of the antechamber — legible only with
the lantern held close — carries half of the inscription:

> HOLLOW MERIDIAN
> THIS TOWER WAS BEGUN **MDCCC**
> *— the rest of the line is broken away —*

The other half is a wedge of the same grey stone on the workbench in the gear gallery,
under the tarpaulin:

> …AND FINISHED **LXXXIX**

Concatenated: **MDCCC + LXXXIX = MDCCCLXXXIX = 1889**.

The tide table is headed `MARCH 1889`, which confirms the century, and the plate's
warning about the reign is there to stop you going looking for a coronation date.

### **1889**

## What the clues are actually testing

Each answer is a three-way join rather than a lookup:

- **Sluice** — one clue names the *rule*, one names the *day*, one supplies a *number*,
  and the units deliberately do not match between the table and the gauge, so you have to
  notice that the footnote is telling you to go somewhere else.
- **Orrery** — the rule comes from a plate in one room, the data from a chart in another,
  and the *definition of the span* only appears in a logbook back in Act I.
- **Vault** — the inscription is physically broken in two and the halves are 70 metres
  and two floors apart, one of them in the dark.
